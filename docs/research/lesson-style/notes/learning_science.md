# Learning-science evidence for lesson writing (research agent report, 15.11.2026, condensed)

[V] = checked against an abstract or reliable secondary source in the session; [M] = from memory, re-check before quoting.

1. **Cognitive load.** Element interactivity drives load (Sweller 2010). Split attention / contiguity: Ginns 2006, 50 studies [V count], d≈0.7–0.85 for novices [M] → define symbols at the point of use. Redundancy costs load (Kalyuga, Chandler & Sweller 1999). Expertise reversal: Kalyuga 2007, doi 10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3 [V].
2. **Mayer principles.** Segmenting: Rey et al. 2019, 56 studies / 88 comparisons, small–medium gains, larger for high prior knowledge [V]. Signaling: Schneider et al. 2018, retention g≈0.53, transfer g≈0.33 [V secondary]. Seductive details: Harp & Mayer 1998 [V secondary], worst at the start of a lesson; Rey 2012: retention d=0.30, transfer d=0.48, illustrations worst, stronger under time limits, only 11/39 studies clearly supportive [V via Tislar & Steelman 2021, PMC8442593]; Sundararajan & Adesope 2020: g≈−0.33 to −0.41, 58 studies, larger for novices and short lessons [V]; a 2026 multilevel meta-analysis g≈−0.16 [V press release]. Boundary conditions: harm under high load / limited time, benefits under low load (Park 2011, 2015), worse with low working memory (Sanchez & Wiley 2006). Personalization: Ginns, Martin & Marsh 2013, d=0.30/0.54, fades in sessions >35 min [V]. Pretraining: Mayer, Mathias & Wetzell 2002 [M].
3. **Narrative and interest.** Willingham 2004, stories are privileged [M]. Mar et al. 2021 (PMC8219577): narrative > expository, g=0.55 (memory 0.72, comprehension 0.48; adults 0.44; I²=98%) [V]. Arya & Maul 2012: discovery narrative more interesting, likely better delayed retention [V design, M result]. History & philosophy of science in teaching (Matthews; Hadzigeorgiou 2006, 2012): interest gains reasonable, conceptual gains weak–moderate. Hidi & Renninger 2006: interest is maintained by meaningful content, not novelty [M].
4. **Worked examples / self-explanation / productive failure.** Worked examples beat unguided problems for novices, with fading (Renkl & Atkinson 2003) [M]. Self-explanation: Bisra et al. 2018 g=0.55 [V]; Hodds, Alcock & Inglis 2014 d=0.95 for proof comprehension [V]. Productive failure: Sinha & Kapur 2021, 53 studies, g=0.36 (0.37–0.58 at high fidelity), gains on conceptual understanding/transfer; requires an accessible attempt, explicit compare-and-contrast, and instruction that builds on the attempts [V].
5. **Retrieval, spacing, interleaving.** Dunlosky et al. 2013: practice testing and distributed practice high utility [M]. Roediger & Karpicke 2006 [M]; Adesope et al. 2017 g≈0.61 [V study, M value]; Cepeda 2006/2008 spacing [M]; Rohrer & Taylor 2007 interleaving [M]. Place checks at segment boundaries (every 5–10 min), ask for generation/prediction.
6. **Reading mathematics.** Inglis & Alcock 2012: novices attend to symbols, experts to the connecting words [V]. Advance organizers d≈0.2 [M]. McNamara et al. 1996: high cohesion helps low-knowledge readers; reverse cohesion effect for high-knowledge readers, mainly passive ones (O'Reilly & McNamara 2007) [V].
7. **Learning styles.** Pashler et al. 2008: no support for meshing [M]. Prior knowledge and content-appropriate dual coding matter.
8. **Screen vs paper.** Delgado et al. 2018: 54 studies, g=−0.21 favoring paper, larger for expository texts (−0.32) and under time pressure, ≈0 for narrative [V].

**Resolution:** narrative and history do not threaten depth when they carry the causal content (the obstacle, the failed model, the constraint). Test: would deleting the sentence delete understanding of why the physics is what it is? Keep and center it if yes; cut or move to an optional side note if no. The learner's conditions (adult, self-paced, motivated, long sessions) all shrink the seductive-details effect.
