# B_eng: an independent body-of-knowledge map for a level-2.5 engineering authority

**What this is.** A reference map of what an engineering authority (level 2.5) has to know, built from sources outside the app (it does not use Gilua's syllabus or books). It has 16 fields, 91 topics and 750 subtopics. Each subtopic has an importance grade, a one-line reason and its evidence.
- Machine-readable map: `B_eng_map.json`, in the same folder. Its schema is `{field: [{topic, subtopics:[{name, importance, why, sources, commonly_missing?}]}]}`.
- Generator (a DSL plus a parser): `scratchpad/bokgen/` (`map1..5.txt`, `parse.py`).

## 1. Method and sources

**Grades:**
- **K (keystone):** high fan-out across fields, needed to judge or critique, or the same structure recurs in several domains.
- **S (supporting):** core inside a field but local to it.
- **C (context):** recognize it, don't study it.
- **N (not needed for this goal):** commonly taught but low leverage for an authority, with the reason given.

**Granularity:** one subtopic ≈ one central result or a tight cluster.

**Reading the numbers:**
- **K = 175.** These are the items a lesson plan should not let go without full level-2.5 treatment: a derivation skeleton, assumptions, limits, orders of magnitude and the cross-domain mapping.
- **S = 485.** Learn each to level 2.5 inside its field, but it doesn't have to be re-derived in every lesson.
- **C + N = 90 (12%).** Candidates for skimming. The N items are commonly taught or examined (many are FE exam-style drills), and the reason for dropping each is in the JSON.
- **The 116 "commonly missing" items** are concentrated in three places: systems engineering (27), structures, materials and design (14), and cross-cutting judgment (12). Physics has none: textbook physics curricula cover physics well.

**The ten gaps with the largest effect on authority** (a judgment call, ranked by fan-out × how often they decide real outcomes):
1. **Units, dimensional consistency and order-of-magnitude estimation**, plus a memorized table of benchmark numbers. This is literally the "mental estimate within 10–20%" in the level-2.5 definition, but no textbook in the stack teaches it as a skill (Mahajan, MIT 6.055).
2. **Floating point and conditioning vs. stability; verification and validation of simulations and mesh convergence; uncertainty quantification.** Every AI answer and every CFD/FEA result has to pass through this filter.
3. **Measurement uncertainty (GUM) and calibration.** Without it, the learner cannot critique test data.
4. **Reliability math, common-cause failures, FMEA and FTA, system safety and STPA.** SEBoK and MIL/NASA standards treat these as core; engineering-science textbooks barely touch them.
5. **Technical budgets and margin policy; interface management.** Integration failures concentrate here, according to NASA-SEH and NASA lessons learned.
6. **Cost estimation, TRL, and cost and schedule growth patterns** (GAO, NASA-CEH). These are essential to critiquing program claims.
7. **Tolerancing and GD&T, stack-up (worst-case vs. RSS), joints and preload, material allowables (A/B basis), factor of safety vs. margin of safety.** All are on FE Mechanical, but absent from strength-of-materials textbooks.
8. **Grounding, shielding and EMC; signal integrity; thermal derating; worst-case circuit analysis.** These are the top real-world electronics failure modes.
9. **Heavy tails, extreme values, multiple comparisons, causal confounding.** These are the statistics needed to critique claims, which differs from the statistics needed for exams.
10. **ML deployment realities: leakage, distribution shift, calibration and OOD detection, compute and scaling laws, reward hacking, sim-to-real, ML assurance.**

## 5. Explicitly not needed for this goal (N), with reasons

These are pruned to save time. Most are FE-style hand drills whose structure is kept elsewhere in the map as K or S.

- **Mathematics foundations**: Measure-theoretic integration (Lebesgue) — not needed at 2.5; know only that it fixes limit interchanges; Differential forms and exterior calculus — elegant but not needed for authority-level engineering; Measure-theoretic probability, sigma-algebras — rigor not needed for 2.5; Catalog of integration techniques (trig substitution drills, partial-fraction drills) — software does it; keep only the structure (residues, partial fractions as pole expansion); Formal epsilon-delta proof writing — read proofs, not write them; 2.5 needs where assumptions enter, not rigor drills; Hand computation of determinants and inverses beyond 3x3 — numerically wrong method anyway; Exact-solution ODE zoo (Bernoulli, Clairaut, exact equations) — rare in engineering; numerical + qualitative methods dominate; Special-function identities (Bessel/Legendre recurrences) — recognize, look up; Contour-integration tricks for real integrals — elegant, low leverage; Statistical tables and manual test procedures — software; keep the logic of tests
- **Physics**: Nonlinear optics — specialist; Quantum field theory — no engineering leverage at this level
- **Signals and systems**: Transform table memorization and long partial-fraction inversions — keep pole-to-time-response intuition only; Analog filter design by hand (component synthesis tables) — tools design; keep the prototype trade-offs
- **Control and estimation**: Root-locus construction rules drilled by hand (breakaway angles etc.) — keep qualitative shape; software plots it; Routh array computation drills — keep meaning; computers find roots
- **Electronics and circuits**: Karnaugh-map and Quine-McCluskey logic minimization — synthesis tools do it; Number-base conversion drills — trivial; know two's complement and floating point instead; Detailed BJT biasing cookbook designs — MOSFET/IC era; keep small-signal idea; Manual Smith-chart matching — keep the concept of reflection and matching
- **Electric machines and power**: Detailed DC machine winding design and commutation — legacy detail; Utility tariff and protection-relay setting procedures — PE practice detail, not authority-level
- **Thermodynamics and heat transfer**: Steam-table interpolation drills — software; keep T-s diagram reasoning; HVAC load calculations and psychrometric chart procedures — building-services specialty
- **Fluids, aerodynamics and propulsion**: Manometer and hydrostatic gate problem drills — exam-style, low leverage; Pipe-network (Hardy Cross) hand iteration — software; keep system-curve logic
- **Structures and materials**: Truss hand-solution drills by joints/sections at scale — keep load-path reasoning; Detailed machine-element catalog selection (keys, couplings, chain sizing) — designer detail; Ferrous metallurgy detail (TTT diagrams of many steels) — keep strength-mechanism concept
- **Machine learning and deep learning**: Framework-specific APIs and coding recipes — change yearly; keep architecture and failure modes; Historical architectures detail (LeNet-to-VGG zoo) — keep the ideas that survived
- **Periphery: discrete math, algorithms, computer systems, chips**: Databases — not core to engineering authority; Compilers — not needed for this goal; Quantum and exotic devices — no current leverage; Competitive-programming data-structure tricks — no engineering-authority leverage; Assembly-language programming — keep ISA concepts only; Network-administration and penetration-testing procedures — operator skill, not engineering judgment

Some of these topics may still be covered inside chapters whose titles don't show it; Ashby, Shigley-like material and NASA-SEH chapters are the likely places. A section-level check is needed before treating any of these as confirmed gaps. Still, the probe matches the expectation in section 3: Gilua's book stack is engineering-science-centric, and most of its likely gaps fall in the practice and judgment layer, not in the core theory.

## 7. Limits of this map
- **Verification.** Grades are my judgment, anchored to the sources. Source codes show where a topic is listed, not a page-level check. Only these were verified verbatim in this run: the FE Electrical & Computer spec, the FE Mechanical spec, the SEBoK table of contents, and the CS2023 knowledge-area list.
- **The ABET AE text came from a draft copy**, so check it against abet.org. The full list of NASA-SEH's 17 processes was not confirmed in this run either.
- **Military-specific topics are excluded** (radar range equation, EW, lethality and similar), because they belong to Lanchester's map. Comms, antennas, guidance and tracking are included only where they are general engineering.
- **Bulk importance numbers are not "time" numbers.** One K item such as the Kalman filter can be worth more hours than five S items.
