### 1.2 Scorecard (1–5, 5 = best)

| Criterion | D1 Concept | D2 Project | D3 Atlas | D4 Fast+Forge | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth retained | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **4.5** |
| Time to finish both | 4 | 1 | 3.5 | 4.5 (by buying it with depth) | 3.5 |
| Authority / judgment | 3 | **5** | 4 | 4 | 4.5 |
| Adherence / joy for *this* learner | 2 | 4.5 | 3.5 | 3 | 4 |
| Build effort (5 = least) | 2 (~22 sessions) | 1 (~38) | 3.5 (11–14 + Phase 0) | 3 (14–17 + Phase 0) | 2.5 (~25–30, staged) |
| Risk (5 = safest) | 2.5 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3.5 |

**Reasoning, briefly.**
- **Depth.** A2 measured the dense format, and J1/J2 checked it blind. It keeps **89–95% of depth units at 55–69% of the words**. The losses are specific and fixable: visuals, two intermediate derivation steps, symbol glosses, one cost-model rationale, and a shared error ("50% costs <2%", which is asymmetric). D1, D3 and D4 all use that format. What separates them is what *follows* reading:
  - D1 checks every node for mastery but flattens the author's voice and the long derivations.
  - D3 adds the assumption delta, which puts *more* words where each field breaks the structure.
  - D4 leaves half the CRs at "read + committed".
  - D2 keeps long lessons and makes closure lessons the place where depth dies, as its own advocate conceded.
- **Time.** See §1.1. D4 is fastest only because it schedules less production. D2 is slowest on every assumption.
- **Authority.** D2's portfolio is the strongest single asset. D3's "one equation, three places it breaks" is the most distinctive article genre. D4 produces something every day. D1 trains components and is weakest on judgment, as its advocate conceded.
- **Joy for this learner** (R2: a builder and writer, visible artefacts, connections, craft, resists gates and grind). D1's 8 nodes a day is the grind he will abandon. D2 matches his identity but crowds out coverage. D3 gives him connections by construction, but months 1–2 are abstract while he is weaker in formal math. D4's forge reads as homework unless it produces artefacts.
- **Risk.** D1: dedup that creates false identities, plus graph complexity. D2: scope creep, a broken build cap, and censor exposure. D3: one wrong structure card contaminates every instance that uses it. D4: the forge gets skipped and D4 decays into "shorter lessons".

## 2. Complementary vs exclusive, and the hybrid

### 2.1 What combines and what must be chosen

**Complementary (take all of them):**
- the dense lesson format (A2 + J1/J2 fixes);
- the nightly factory (A3);
- the CR ledger with teach-once dedup (D1), plus the structure atlas as a layer of `same-structure` edges over that ledger (D3). These are the same graph;
- forge tasks (D4) and projects (A6). A project milestone *is* a forge task;
- encompassing review credit (D1) and forge credit (D4);
- the page-coverage ledger (D1), the instance map (D3) and the 3-state map (D4). These are one object.

**Mutually exclusive (one choice each):**

| Choice | Options | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering principle | project-JIT (D2) / atlas phase first (D3) / graph frontier (D1) | **Graph frontier in syllabus spirit**, plus A6's Lanchester reorder (P0 → P1 → M2.1–2.15 → P6 → M7.1–7.3 → P13 orbits → rest of P2), plus each next flagship's closure pulled forward. Full JIT is rejected: it creates closure debt. |
| Atlas as a phase | 220–270 standalone lessons up front / embedded | **Embedded.** Gilua P0–P10 *are* the atlas texts. The ~40 highest-fan-out structures get a structure card and a concrete-first opening inside the module where they first appear. No two-month abstraction tax. |
| Lesson length rule | ≥4,000-word floor (V2, D2) / budget per CR (A1, A2) | **Budget per CR plus a unit-coverage proof.** A 2-CR lesson lands at 4–5k words anyway, which is R4's core band. A 1-CR lesson is allowed to be 2.5k. Only the learner can lift his floor. Show him the J1/J2 evidence and let him decide. |
| Granularity | 25–50-min nodes, 8 a day (D1) / module lessons | **Lessons of 1–2 CRs inside their module.** This keeps the sense of place and the author's voice, and avoids flash-card atomisation. |
| Project load | 10 heavy (480 h, D2) / 8 with 3 flagships (~110 h, A6) | **A6's 8.** D2's ten do not fit any calendar. |

### 2.2 The hybrid: "Ledger, Lesson, Forge"

**Unit of progress.** The **central result** in one cross-app ledger (A3's concept registry, extended with D1's edges: `prereq`, `same-structure`, `used-by`, `encompasses`). Each CR has three states:
- **covered**: the lesson was opened, the lint shows every planned unit present, and the commits were made;
- **forged**: production work was done on it;
- **proven**: it passed an unannounced delayed check.

Modules and parts remain the *view*: ring map, part progress. CRs are the *accounting*.

**Lesson format.** A2's template with the J1/J2 guardrails:
- thread question → `definition` block → derivation skeleton with **assumption:** marked in bold at its step → commit-first `think` immediately before the step it tests → formula with domain of validity → anchors to remember → trade-off, limits and critique as claim-first paragraphs → one cross-field table → 3-line `keep`.
- Guardrails: every symbol is named the first time it appears; intermediate results are never skipped; scenes, charts and figures are kept (they cost no prose); errata only for *this* topic's formulas; one-line "used before defined" notes.
- Lint at 25–35 words per unit, fail above 45.
- In Lanchester instances, an **assumption delta** (D3) listing each structure assumption as holds, bent or broken. Every bent or broken assumption is taught in full.
- **Depth layers** hold full derivations (about 80 "derivation lessons" where the derivation *is* the content), "read the original" cards for about 60 Feynman/Kolmogorov passages, and history.

**Dedup.** Teach once, at the MID level that A1 measured. The factory merges two results only when their *equations* match. Otherwise it creates a `same-structure` edge with a "where the analogy breaks" paragraph. The headline case is Lanchester P0 (M0.1–M0.7): it becomes 1–2-hour delta lessons placed after the matching Gilua modules.

**Forge** (D4, 45–60 min/day, first thing in the day). Reconstructions, estimate sets, design reviews of *real* faults, black boxes, and project steps. Prioritised by fan-out × spike × not-yet-proven. Each task touches 2–4 CRs. Every CR still gets A1's in-lesson active work: a commit, a "your step", and an estimate.

**Review.** One item per CR (two for spike CRs), seeded from his "remember this" markers in the summaries notebook (R2, R4). Expanding intervals of 1–3–7–21–60 days. Forge and project use count as encompassing credit. A hard cap of about 25 min/day. Overflow retires to a module-end check, and the warm-up says how many were retired. Steady state is about 8 new items/day × ~5 reviews − ~35% credit ≈ 26 reviews/day, which fits.

**Projects.** A6's calendar:
- P12 claims checker + P01 link lab in weeks 1–2;
- P04 salvo mini in weeks 4–5;
- **Flagship A** Radar–EW Budget Lab, weeks 6–8;
- **Flagship B** Track-to-Intercept, weeks 10–12;
- **Flagship C** Interceptor Arithmetic, weeks 13–15;
- P08 satellites, a choice slot, the C-UAS capstone, and a portfolio week.

In every project, *he writes the physics, the predictions, the validation against book examples and the article*, and Claude Code writes the plumbing. Projects run on the consolidation day plus 2–3 evenings, about 110 h in total. Each publication passes A6 §6's framing gate.

**Factory vs page.**

| Nightly factory (cloud, Opus, Python) | In the page |
|---|---|
| CR extraction, page ledger, dedup and edges; structure cards | Opening ready lessons, commits, "done" as the learner's act |
| Lessons per A3: analyst → architect → writer → V1–V5 verifiers + senior reviewer; keys, review items, layers | Live fallback "כתוב עכשיו", clarify, Explain (Haiku) |
| Forge queue and task generation (keys hidden); project validation tables | Forge workspace, review warm-up, grading against Opus keys (default tier, Opus on dispute) |
| Memory projection from the registry; nightly patching of unopened lessons | Map (proven / connections), coverage page, summaries notebook, sound and look |
| Usage cap `FACTORY_MAX`; `needs-live` on refusal | Usage counter and events log |

**Day (5 h base; the learner may choose 6–7):** review 20 → forge 50 → break → lessons 1–2 (2 CRs each, about 55 min each) → break → lesson 3 → notebook from memory 15 → close. About **6 CRs a day**. Short day (2 h): review + forge 30 + 1 lesson.

**Week:** 5 lesson days + 1 project/consolidation day (bridge note, article) + 1 rest day. A light week every 7th week (R4).

**Coverage proof.**
1. A page ledger per module: every numbered equation, figure, table and worked example maps to a CR, a support unit, a context fold, or skip-with-reason. A blind Opus pass audits 10% of it, and its error rate is published.
2. A unit list per lesson, with planned = present.
3. A monthly held-out exam per spike, drawn from excluded pages and computed items (V2, R4).

## 3. Stress tests

**(a) "Finish both, no depth loss, a few months, with projects."** This cannot be met as stated. A1's frontier puts both curricula at full depth with projects in 6 months at **10–11.5 h/day**. The hybrid improves on S7 by only about 5%, because the big levers (dense lessons, the factory, dedup) are already in A1's S5–S7. What *can* be met:
- **the core of both, at full depth, with all 8 projects, in 7.4 months at 6 h/day or 6.3 months at 7 h/day**;
- then the adv tier (about 450 CRs) as a phase of about 3 months, or as deltas pulled in by projects.

That is a trade on *timing*, not on depth.

**(b) A1's frontier.** The hybrid sits on the S7 row: 8 months for both needs 7.5 h/day (P50) or 8.5 (P80); 12 months needs 5.0–6.0. Core only (S9): 6 months needs 7.5 h/day, 8 months needs 6.0. Above about 7 h/day of dense first-time study, A1 warns that yield falls, and that is not modelled. So **the realistic fast plan is 6–7 h/day**. The 8-month "both" plan sits at the edge of what a person can sustain.

**(c) Refusals.**
- The factory works in CR-sized units with narrow briefs in the 28.10 engineering register, so a refusal costs one lesson (marked `needs-live`), not a day.
- The shelf holds a 1.5–2-day buffer.
- Flagship C and P05 carry the highest exposure. They are framed as generic textbook OR (A6 §6), and their milestone prompts are probed before the week they start.
- Refusals are never evaded by rephrasing. The diagnostic record goes to `profile/diag/` for him to decide.
- If the Lanchester refusal rate exceeds 5% of lessons, its instance lessons revert to in-page generation with the existing protections.

**(d) Usage limits.**
- A factory lesson costs about 1.5–2.5 U (A3). At 3 lessons/day that is about 5–7 U a night, against today's ~1 U per lesson in the page. Daytime use falls to clarify, grading and Explain, so the net is about 1.5–2× today's total.
- If the weekly cap binds, the degradation ladder is:
  1. drop the senior reviewer and V2 page-image checks for non-spike CRs (→ ~1.1 U);
  2. shrink the shelf horizon to 1 day;
  3. as a last resort, live generation with the dense rules.
- Depth rules never degrade; only the verification depth does. A usage counter is a Phase 0 item.

**(e) Motivation in month 3.** This is where the risks gather: the novelty is gone, the review queue is at steady state, and the first long technical stretch (Gilua P5–P8) is under way. Mitigations:
- the calendar puts **Flagships B and C in weeks 10–15**, the exact months at risk;
- the visible map turns "proven" and the connection roads darker each week;
- at least 3 publications are out by week 15;
- the review cap with retirement means a backlog never accumulates;
- short-day mode and the light week.

Early-warning triggers, checked weekly:
- adherence <80% of planned hours for 2 weeks;
- forge skipped >30% of days;
- 3 consecutive ratings of "long" or "shallow".

Response: switch to the fallback day (forge 30, review 15, 3 lessons, project evening), and at a month-3 checkpoint let him re-choose the spike, as R4 recommends.

**(f) The dense format still loses depth.** The measured loss is 5–11% of units. It sits in identifiable places (visuals, intermediate steps, glosses), and every one of those has a mechanical guard:
- the unit list lint;
- the number-fidelity check (A2 caught a dropped anchor this way);
- the "every symbol named" rule;
- visuals that do not count against the word budget.

The residual risk is that a *missed unit at planning* is invisible to the lint. Guard against it with the page ledger audit and the pilot's delayed A/B test. **If dense lessons score below 95% of long ones on a 10-day closed-book reconstruction, raise the budget to 35–40 words per unit.** That costs about +30% reading, or +150–200 h, which is about 1.3 months at 5 h/day.

**Honest final trade-off.** No design finishes both curricula, at full level-2.5 depth with spaced review and projects, in under about 8 months without 7.5+ hours of study a day. The binding constraint is the learner's own minutes per central result multiplied by about 1,700 central results, not the tutor, the format or generation speed. What the hybrid buys is the minimum of that product, with nothing taught shallower: about 1,400 hours instead of the status quo's 2,730, with roughly 4× the retention (0.85 vs 0.21). He chooses the calendar:
- **both curricula in about 8 months at 7 h/day**;
- **or the core of both in about 7.4 months at 6 h/day**, adv deferred to a second phase;
- **or everything in about 12 months at 5 h/day**.

Each has the same depth, the same 8 projects and the same proof of coverage. The only cut on the table is a *timing* cut (adv later), and he makes it knowingly, after two weeks of measurements replace the four biggest assumptions.
