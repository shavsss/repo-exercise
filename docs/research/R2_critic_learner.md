# R2. The learner's advocate: critique of Vision v1

Stance: I speak for the person who will open these two apps every morning. I read V1 in full and skimmed C (lesson critique), D (landscape), and the plan documents (`docs/CORE_LAB_PLAN.md`, `docs/ENG_LAB_PLAN.md`). V1 is right on the science. As written, though, he will reject about a third of it on sight. Most of that third he would accept if it were framed and sequenced differently. This critique is about that gap.

---

## 1. What he will love, what he will resist, and where V1 collides with him

### He will love these

- **The seven-point operational definition of "authority" (§1).** This is his own goal stated better than he has stated it. "Catch a planted error in a fluent text", "run a sharp expert interview" and "say where the analogy breaks" describe what he does as a writer and interviewer. This section should open everything we show him.
- **The practitioner layer, the red-team paragraph and "numbers to remember".** All three add material, so he reads them as more depth. A professional misconception, or the interview question with "what a strong answer contains", is exactly the kind of thing he saves to the notebook as an article or interview item.
- **Judgment trainers, especially for Lanchester.** A war-game turn, decision cases cut at the decision point, and forecasts with resolution dates all feel like his home ground (military analysis). They are also the most enjoyable parts of V1.
- **The cross-app concept graph and the weekly "bridge" session.** He has always treated the two labs as one project (same look, same notebook code, shared scene3d). If the graph shows link budget ↔ signals and Lanchester laws ↔ ODEs, he gets the "cross-domain connections" he called "the most important thing", in visible form.
- **The article and interview pipeline (§7).** It is the first feature aimed at his public output rather than his private study.

### He will resist, and V1 must reframe these

### What he will abandon after a week, even if he accepts it

- **The daily close showing "the 7 numbers' movement".** Seven abstract scores that barely move day to day are discouraging. Show one or two numbers daily and all seven weekly (see §2).
- **Teach-back to a "confused student" persona.** He is not a teacher. He writes articles and interviews experts. Recast the same mechanism as *explaining to a sceptical editor* or *answering a sharp interviewer* and it fits his identity, so he'll keep doing it.
- **Black-box simulators as a separate drill.** They are good, but as a standalone daily item they turn into a chore. Embed them in lessons as the "think" for the relevant result.
- **Typed answers for every think block.** Typing a full answer in Hebrew with equations at every think, 5 lessons a day, is heavy friction. Allow a *number*, a *choice*, or a *one-line* commitment by default, with a longer answer optional. The rule is "commit to something", not "write an essay".

## 2. Is the daily loop realistic for him?

Not as written. Five consecutive blocks, 4.5–5 h, every day for two months, all self-scheduled, is a plan for a disciplined student with no other life. He is a builder and a writer. What keeps him going is the craft, the visible artefact and the feeling of rising level. What would make him actually do the retrieval and production parts every day:

1. **Zero added friction: retrieval lives where he already is.** The warm-up is the first three paragraphs of the lesson he wants to read anyway, not a separate screen. The judgment item is the last think block of the last lesson of the day. The authority block is the notebook, which he already uses. Every separate screen is a place he can skip.
2. **Make it sound and look as good as the rest.** He asked for mechanical-keyboard sounds, themed cursors, cream backgrounds and coloured blocks. Retrieval items need the same care: a typed commitment that "locks" with a satisfying click, a red marker stroke when he finds the planted error, a calibration dial that fills like an instrument gauge. Joy is not decoration for this learner. It is what got him to build three apps.
3. **Visible progress he cares about.** He doesn't care about a "retention %". He cares about **level and connections**. So show:
   - the ring map filling not only with "read" but with "proven" (a second, darker fill);
   - a **connections map** that grows as cross-domain items are answered correctly: the graph of his own expertise;
   - a weekly **"what I can now do closed-book"** list in Hebrew, e.g. "derive the radar equation skeleton; estimate the horizon range of a 10 m mast within 20%";
   - a calibration curve that tightens over weeks. It's an instrument, and he likes instruments.
4. **Production that pays off outside the app.** Every "authority" exercise should leave a reusable artefact: a paragraph in the article pipeline, a question in the interview bank, a claim with a source in the claims table. If the work disappears into a score, he will stop. If it accumulates into his next article, he will keep doing it.
5. **A "short day" mode.** On days with 2 hours, the app should offer the highest-value 2 hours (one lesson, plus the opening retrieval and the due judgment item), not guilt. A plan that only works on perfect days fails within two weeks.

## 3. What V1 misses about his real goals

1. **Public authority is the end, and study is the means.** V1 puts "Produce" as item 7 of 7 and §7 as Phase 3. For him it is the *reason* for the whole project. Two consequences:
   - Some of the authority pipeline belongs in Phase 1, cheaply: the "interview question + strong answer" line in the practitioner layer, and inline display of saved article/interview items at lesson end (C's small item).
   - **Accuracy is reputational for him, not academic.** One wrong number in a published article costs more than a week of lessons. The verification pass and errata log (V1 §8, Phase 3) should move up, and the claims table should trace every number to computed / page / memory. C found three factual errors in five lessons, and that matters most for this learner.
2. **Two apps, one intellectual project.** V1 mentions a shared concept graph but plans the apps in parallel. He thinks of himself as one person building one kind of expertise: military systems understood through engineering and mathematics. The vision should say so explicitly. There is one learner model and one calibration log. Lanchester lessons cite the Gilua result he has already proven ("you derived the radar equation in M4.2; here it decides the SAM engagement envelope"), and vice versa. *That* is the rising cross-domain level he demands, and it costs nothing in new material.
3. **The summaries notebook is his main artefact, and V1 ignores it.** He built it with care (equation ribbon, proof lists, boxes, spell-check, nightly backup). It is the one thing he *produces himself*, which is exactly what learning science says works. V1 builds a new learner model and a new review queue next to it. Instead:
   - review items should **come from his summaries** (D's RemNote idea: a "remember this" marker on any line, formula or definition box);
   - the tutor should **read his summary** of a module and comment on gaps before the oral exam;
   - the article pipeline should start from the summaries, not from a new pipeline.

   Writing the module summary *is* the generation exercise. It just needs to be scheduled and fed back.
4. **His time, including build time.** He builds these apps himself with an AI assistant. V1's roadmap is a large engineering programme (learner model, FSRS, concept graph, shared monorepo, pre-generation, verification pass, judgment sims). Every week spent building is a week not studying, and the 2-month clock is running. The vision needs a **builder's budget**: which features pay back their build time within the 2 months? Phase 1 should be the subset that is mostly prompt and rule changes plus one or two small UI components, so the study plan doesn't stall.
5. **His strength is military judgment, and his weakness is formal math.** V1 adapts "by node state" in general terms. Specifically: in Lanchester, retrieval and judgment can be lighter and faster because he is already strong there. In Gilua, his recent feedback (definitions before examples, a clear thread, key formulas vs illustrations) shows the math needs *more* structure, not fewer words. The concrete adaptation is "more worked structure in math, more judgment cases in military".

## 4. Presenting the hard truths honestly and persuasively

He asked to be challenged "without limits". So don't soften the truths. Lead with his own goal and give him the data and the control.

- **Lead with his goal, not our diagnosis.** Start with the seven-point definition of authority and ask "is this what you want to be able to do?" Every hard truth then becomes an obstacle to *his* goal, not a criticism of his apps.
- **Show the numbers he can check.** Show C's table (words per lesson, 5–9 h/day of reading) and the retention estimate. Then show the honest page count: 4,150 pages in 60 days is ~70 pages/day. Present it as arithmetic, not opinion.
- **Credit what he built.** C says the lessons contain passages "as good as a strong professor's", with ~70 numbers checked and holding. Say so first. The message is "the engine is world-class; the gearbox is missing", not "the lessons are too long".
- **Offer options, not a verdict, for the schedule.** Three honest paths:
  - (a) 2 months, with tiered modules: core at full depth, others as a triage lesson with full depth on demand;
  - (b) 4–5 months at full depth for everything;
  - (c) 2 months with today's format, knowing that retention will be low.

  Let him choose. V1 §9 already says "the learner decides". Make that the headline, and add that the app will show the *real* pace from his data after two weeks, so the decision can be revisited with evidence.
- **Cite the AI-tutor evidence, because it is about him.** Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025) found that AI that explains raised practice scores and *lowered* unaided performance. A tutor that holds the answer and gives hints removed the harm. He wants "the best, the most correct". This is the most correct finding in the field, and it argues for *more* Opus involvement in his reasoning, not less.
- **Make every change reversible and visible.** Depth layers open with one click and can be set to "always open". The coverage proof is in every lesson. The mastery gate is soft. With these, the "no reduction" promise is something he can verify, not just trust.
- **Don't say these words:** "budget", "cut", "shorter lessons", "cheap model", "spaced repetition", "gate". Say instead: core thread and depth layers, coverage proof, Opus-keyed checks, built-in recall, proof of mastery.

## 5. The five most important changes to the vision

1. **Replace "scope budget" with "core thread ≥ 4,000 words + depth layers + automatic coverage proof".** This keeps his word floor and his no-cut rule, makes the cut-free promise verifiable, and still delivers C's fix (one or two central results, a clear thread, extras moved into layers rather than deleted).
2. **Put his summaries notebook at the centre of the loop.** Review items come from "remember this" markers in his summaries. The module's closing production task is writing or completing his summary, which the tutor then critiques. The article and interview pipeline starts from it. Don't build a parallel artefact.
3. **Reframe mastery and retrieval as acceleration, and reopen the no-SRS decision openly with data.** Recall is built into the lesson opening and into cross-domain exercises (no separate deck). The soft "proven" marker unlocks faster, shorter future lessons. The FSRS machinery stays invisible.
4. **Move authority and accuracy forward.** Phase 1 should add the interview question + strong answer, inline display of saved article/interview items, a verified-numbers check on key figures, and an errata log. Recast teach-back as "explain it to a sceptical editor or interviewer". Treat both apps as one project, with one learner model, one calibration log and explicit cross-app callbacks in lessons.
5. **Design the loop for a real person: same hours, less reading, more doing, and make it a pleasure.** Show the old and new day side by side in minutes. Allow light commitments (a number, a choice, one line). Add a short-day mode. Show weekly visible progress (proven ring, connections map, "what I can do closed-book", calibration gauge). Give the new interactions the same sensory craft as the rest of the app. Add a builder's budget, so Phase 1 is mostly rules and small components and studying starts again within days, not weeks.
